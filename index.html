9-Pillar Engineer Growth Framework

A spider chart to measure software engineering mastery, identify core interests, and guide career paths. Supported by a 45-point competency matrix across 5 proficiency levels. By Jasper Loo Zhu Hang.

The nine pillars

Each pillar is rated L1 to L5. The nine group into three clusters:

Technical

  • Coding (Hands) — implementation, data structures, refactoring, testing discipline.
  • Domain Logic (Nose) — business rules, workflow integrity, edge cases and loopholes.
  • Architecture (Brain) — system boundaries, data modelling, API design, secure design.
  • AI Leverage (Machine) — using AI tooling to multiply engineering output.

Product

  • UI/UX (Eyes) — interface quality, interaction and visual judgement.
  • Product Sense (Gut) — user need, prioritisation, product trade-offs.

Operational

  • Process (Spine) — delivery discipline, planning, engineering hygiene.
  • Communication (Voice) — written and verbal clarity, alignment, influence.
  • Ownership (Soul) — accountability, follow-through, raising the bar.

The five proficiency levels

Pillar levels are written L1 to L5 and career stages S1 to S5, kept separate on purpose: S3 does not mean L3 in every pillar. Career stage reads from the whole chart shape, not from any single axis.

  1. L1 Learner — can follow clear instructions, match existing patterns, and execute with high support.
  2. L2 Practitioner — can complete defined tasks and workflows end-to-end independently.
  3. L3 Expert — can navigate messy, complex problems alone while meeting standard expectations.
  4. L4 Mentor — can raise the quality bar for others, resolve cross-team friction, mentor peers, and hold the team to the foundations below them.
  5. L5 Strategist — can navigate high ambiguity, solve systemic risks, shape direction beyond the team, and set the foundations the wider company inherits.
